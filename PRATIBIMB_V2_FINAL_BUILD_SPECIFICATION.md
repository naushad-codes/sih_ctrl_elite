# PRATIBIMB V2 — Final Build Specification

**Purpose:** Research-backed product specification, laptop-only prototype architecture, implementation contract and judge-demonstration plan.  
**Date:** 30 September 2026 · **Status:** design, not implemented or validated.  
**PS 26060, team-supplied wording:** “Digital Platform for efficient remote management of Indian Antarctic Research Stations”; develop a Digital Twin framework for Maitri and Bharati integrating infrastructure, energy, logistics and environmental monitoring. MoES/NCPOR · Software · Smart Automation. Archive the official PS record in the team's portal. Official SIH 2026 guidelines describe novelty, complexity, clarity, feasibility, practicability, sustainability, impact, user experience and progression as selection criteria, **without PS-specific numeric weights** [S01].

> **Executive research summary:** There is a defensible middle ground between a dashboard and remote actuation. An authorized ground team can remotely **coordinate operational work**—request a reading, submit a proposed response, track acknowledgement and execution reports, plan resources, and verify outcomes—without a software path to a generator or pump. Public NCPOR material describes existing technical-issue management and logistics planning functions [S04][S05], which means PRATIBIMB should **complement, not claim to invent or replace**, these workflows. Public weather archives exist, but no approved current station operations telemetry was found [S06][S07]. Therefore use a clearly marked station emulator and historical weather replay to prove the *software workflow*, not operational accuracy. The decisive innovation is the closed loop **scenario → plan → station acknowledgement → reported observation → model revision and outcome check**, with auditable uncertainty and real offline delivery.

**Vocabulary:** `REMOTE OPERATIONS MANAGER` = people/tasks/plans/evidence, not actuators. `Station emulator` = demonstrator generating synthetic station state. `Twin` = model of *the emulated station* in this demo plus a station-configurable framework intended for future authorized observation. When discussing real Bharati/Maitri, call it a **twin prototype**, not a calibrated replica. No feature is named with another Hindi brand; PRATIBIMB is the product name.

## 1. Critical audit of V1 and research conclusions

**What V1 got right:** station-specific profiles, coupled power–heat–fuel–water model, source labels, immutable scenario forks, edge operation, event replication, Measure Next, Robust Action Check, Projection Check, explicit no-actuation boundary and honest calibration limits. Preserve all of those.

**What V1 got wrong or left weak:**
1. **Remote management was underdeveloped.** The ground site mostly watched, ran scenarios and wrote notes. There was no first-class task, recipient, delivery, acknowledgement, verification, escalation or closed-loop record. A judge could fairly call it a simulator plus dashboard.
2. **No independent demo reality.** If both telemetry and predictions are generated by the same function, a post-reconnect prediction check only confirms the team's own equation. V2 separates the station emulator from the forecaster, includes hidden fault/parameter perturbations and can replay independently obtained historical weather; it still does **not** claim physical-station validation.
3. **No explicit local-vs-remote authority.** Ground requests must be proposals, not directives to execute. Local staff may reject, defer, request clarification or record an alternative action; no local workflow can be mistaken for an industrial interlock.
4. **Feature-order risk.** V1 placed complex sensitivity features before a complete management transaction. V2 first ships the end-to-end **plan–ack–report–verify** path and then adds sophistication.
5. **Unsafe language risk:** “robust” means under *tested declared cases*, not safe; Projection Check is a discrepancy indicator, not failure diagnosis; station capacity in a historic tender is not live inventory [S02][S03][S14].

### Research findings that drive this redesign

- NCPOR describes Maitri's fuel infrastructure and lake-water pump house [S02]; a 2013 Bharati maintenance tender describes three 100-kVA CHP units, Jet-A1 supply through a day tank, seawater intake and a building management system [S03]. These are **dated published descriptions**, not present equipment status, available kW or current usage.
- NCPOR says a Bharati-TIMES web application was developed and used for technical life-support issues [S04]. A past NCPOR logistics-software RFP lists expedition plans, yearly supply demands and cargo tracking [S05]. **Whether either system is in current service, its APIs or integration policy are not established from available public evidence.** Differentiate on linked scenario evidence and offline plan-result reconciliation; propose future export/integration, not replacement.
- NCPOR's polar data center lists historical meteorological data for both Maitri and Bharati [S06]; AntAWS has 267 AWS records spanning 1980–2021 with uneven completeness [S07]. ERA5 is reanalysis, not a station measurement [S08]. No current life-support feed is implied by weather archives.
- A peer-reviewed study of Germany's Neumayer III models Antarctic CHP, heat, power, storage and renewables, supporting **coupled modeling as an engineering approach** [S09]. Its parameters are **not** Bharati/Maitri parameters. NIST argues that digital-twin decisions depend on verification, validation and uncertainty quantification [S10]. Hence validate equations and software behavior, and state station-fidelity limits.
- BAS's Halley automation maintains science instrumentation and fuel monitoring during unmanned periods [S11]; Belgium's Princess Elisabeth runs priority-based smart-grid management [S12]. These demonstrate polar remote operations are possible under *their* engineering approvals, **not** authority to remotely actuate Indian equipment.
- Azure IoT Edge documents local message store/forward with storage/TTL limits [S13]. AWS device shadows distinguish desired from reported state [S15], but **their desired state can command a device**. PRATIBIMB instead uses `PROPOSED_PLAN` and `STATION_REPORTED_OUTCOME` as workflow records, with no automatic mapping to equipment.
- NCPOR described both stations as having high-speed Internet in an expedition call [S16], while a 2013 five-day Bharati communication outage was caused by earth-station power being switched off [S17]. Offline resilience is defensible without inventing routine weather blackouts or numeric link bandwidth. Seasonal ship operations depend on the voyage plan [S18].
- NASA documents hardware-in-the-loop as testing embedded interfaces with simulated plants [S19]. A laptop-only software-in-the-loop station emulator tests *our integration* at lower cost; an optional bench ESP32 tests ingress, not actual station fidelity.

**Interpretation, not official policy:** The PS's “remote management” can reasonably mean *remote coordination of observation, planning, maintenance/logistics requests and verified follow-up*. The PS does not explicitly require actuator commands. This interpretation must be checked with the sponsoring organization if clarification is possible; no NCPOR SOP, approval chain, integration access or user role assignment is claimed here.

**Excluded:** pretending a CSV is live telemetry; a randomized UI chart with no persisted events; training on simulator faults and calling the model validated; large ESP32/relay rigs; real sensor requirement for passing the demo. **Choice:** two local station-emulator/edge instances plus a separate HQ process on one laptop; official historical weather replay if rights allow, otherwise a labelled bundled synthetic weather sequence; manual readings and tasks; deterministic fault injection. No IoT device required. Software-only costs mainly laptop and development time, with optional free/local dependencies; exact costs depend on team's equipment/licenses.

### Digital-twin and offline alternatives
- **Static 3D/SCADA mirror:** easy but does not predict consequences or close tasks. Reject as product core.
- **Pure mathematical simulator:** useful but does not maintain observed/reported state, provenance or reconciliation. Embed inside revisioned twin.
- **Cloud-only twin:** easy multi-user demo but dies under link loss and needs Internet. Reject for critical demo.
- **Stateful edge + ground replica with scenario fork:** chosen; more engineering but laptop feasible, explicit state ownership and testable failure conditions.
- **Browser localStorage offline toggle:** easy theater, no durable backend or event semantics. Reject.
- **SQLite event journal + idempotent HTTP batches:** chosen; deliberate P0 limit to two station peers and HQ; not claiming general-purpose distributed consensus.

## 4. FINAL ARCHITECTURAL DECISION

1. **What:** PRATIBIMB V2 is a **remote operations workflow linked to a digital-twin prototype**, not a dashboard with attached tickets.
2. **Twin requirement:** revisioned, station-specific reported state; causal model; forked what-if; conditional future; evidence/assumption ledger; later observation reconciliation. Its emulated-station behavior is demonstrable; real-station accuracy is unproven [S10].
3. **Remote management:** HQ creates a signed-in-app **non-executable work proposal** (measurement/inspection/plan/logistics request) after scenario comparison; station receives, accepts/rejects, reports its human decision/observation, and HQ reviews an outcome. Real NCPOR approval policies are not claimed.
4. **Without sensors:** feed **DEMO_SYNTHETIC** operational signals from a separate emulated station; replay labelled **HISTORICAL_WEATHER**; accept **MANUAL_DEMO_ENTRY**; later read-only approved integrations are future work.
5. **Simulated:** plant operation, fuel/inventory level, generator status, fault occurrences, task responses in laptop demo. A human participant can act as station user rather than automatic fake replies.
6. **Historical:** attributed AWS records and dated public station documentation, never current operating values.
7. **Operator-entered:** demonstrated fuel reading, checklist result, proposed supply date and acceptance; all manually labelled.
8. **Actually demonstrated:** software-in-the-loop causal calculation, work-plan delivery/acknowledgement, local autonomy, reconnect reconciliation and held projection comparison. No real plant integration or safety authorization.
9. **Not claimed:** present Bharati/Maitri fidelity, live sensor access, generator RUL, measured fuel savings, automatic generator control, approved station SOP, secure government deployment or guaranteed satellite behavior.
10. **Single judge proof:** judge supplies an unexpected load or fuel value, HQ sends a `MEASUREMENT_REQUEST`/`INSPECTION_TASK` based on a contingency; station accepts and records a differing measurement while disconnected; reconnect reconciles and the earlier conditional prediction is checked, revised and audited.

**The central loop:** `station state → event/incident → model fork → first constraint → feasible candidate → measurement/plan request → queued delivery → local human response → observed outcome → reconciliation → projection check → updated plan/audit`.

## 5. Product behavior and remote operations transaction

### 5.1 Scope and ownership
HQ may **request a reading, propose a contingency plan, request inspection, record supply intent, ask for evidence and review results**. It cannot demand an actual equipment switch via machine-readable command. Station-side staff own operational observations and their own execution reports. In the prototype, role names are **illustrative**; NCPOR's true authority matrix is not established from public evidence. Ground shall never overwrite station readings. A station response may say `DECLINED` or `CANNOT_VERIFY` with a reason.

### 5.2 Remote Operations Manager: non-cosmetic workflow

**Objects:** `Incident`, `ScenarioResult`, `ActionPlan`, `Task`, `MeasurementRequest`, `InventoryAdjustment`, `OutcomeReport`, `EvidenceAttachment`, `Review`, `SyncReceipt`. Every plan links base station revision, model version, reason, candidate action, constraints, uncertainty, expected *conditional* outcome, recipient role, expiry/review-by time, created-at and provenance IDs. Avoid attachments containing actual sensitive data in public demo.

**Action plan state machine:** `DRAFT → REVIEWED_FOR_COMMUNICATION → QUEUED → DELIVERED → SEEN → ACCEPTED | DECLINED | CLARIFICATION_REQUESTED → IN_PROGRESS → REPORTED_COMPLETE | REPORTED_BLOCKED → REVIEWED_OUTCOME → CLOSED | REOPENED`. `EXPIRED` is a terminal/exception state for undelivered or unaccepted plans after defined expiry, **not** proof unsafe action. Transition graph is enforced server-side with role + current-revision preconditions. Station receives **textual proposal and required checks**, never an actuator command. P0 can implement only `DRAFT, QUEUED, DELIVERED, ACCEPTED/DECLINED, REPORTED_COMPLETE/BLOCKED, REVIEWED_OUTCOME`; other states P1.

**Task types P0:** `MEASUREMENT_REQUEST(metric,unit,reason,deadline)`, `INSPECTION_REQUEST(asset,requested_checks,reason)`, `PLAN_REVIEW(scenario_id,candidate_action,constraints)`, `INVENTORY_VERIFICATION(item,unit)`. None mandate unsafe action. Local response can include observed reading with measured/manual/demo label, performed work note, evidence time, disposition and reason. A report is **self-reported**, never falsely marked independently verified. HQ review contrasts outcome and scenario expectation, resolves/reopens, and creates follow-up request.

**Acceptance criteria:** A plan exists as an event separate from scenario result; after actual network block it stays `QUEUED` at HQ and does not appear at edge; after reconnect edge receipt yields `DELIVERED`, station user manually accepts/rejects; a reply returns; results update the twin, invalidating old forecasts. Merely clicking an animated sync toggle fails this gate.

### 5.3 What if HQ is wrong or unreachable?
A local user may enter a new observation, run a local scenario, decline a stale incoming plan, or create a local incident without HQ. Before presenting a newly delivered proposal, station UI compares `base_revision` and model/input age; if superseded, mark `STALE_PLAN` and require local re-review. HQ cannot silently amend an already accepted plan; new plan revision and local acknowledgement required. The tool cannot enforce real-world safety; physical work follows whatever authorized station procedures exist outside this prototype.

### 5.4 Resource and environment management
Resource planner uses operator-entered stock and replenishment intervals, dependency-aware consumption and unresolved gap. It can produce an `INVENTORY_VERIFICATION` or `SUPPLY_REVIEW` proposal; no auto-order. Environmental screen shows attributed historical outdoor temperature/wind and what portion drives modeled thermal demand. Weather is contextual/forcing, not an invented real-time severe-weather alert. Other interventions—fire, medical, aircraft or SAR—are out of MVP.

## 6. Station emulator, replay and twin model

### 6.1 Independent station emulator (“demo world”)

Implement a **separate process/package** `station_world` with its own private internal state and versioned manifest. It generates coherent time series from a fixed seed, scheduled disturbances and simple conservation rules: generator availability/output, critical/deferrable load, fuel L, indoor/outdoor °C, water production/storage L, inventory, connectivity and timestamps. It emits a subset as **DEMO_SYNTHETIC_OBSERVATION**, like an imperfect instrument: sampling lag, gaps, contradictory/poor-quality values, missingness and controlled measurement noise. Use a distinct implementation/parameter fixture from the decision model; where possible include a hidden efficiency/heat-loss perturbation or unsignaled fuel-transfer event. Do **not** simply call the forecast function as the “truth” generator: that makes Projection Check circular. Still, agreement with an emulator only validates *the workflow under tested simulated conditions*, not Bharati.

Weather can be an attributed historical replay file (NCPOR NPDC if accessible/reusable [S06], otherwise AntAWS [S07]) or explicit synthetic substitute. Record original date/site, replay clock and resampling method. A 2015 recorded outdoor temperature presented on a 2026 demo clock remains **historical replay**, never live 2026 weather. The scenario engine can inject normal, CHP unavailability, critical load spike, temperature change, water production degradation, communication loss, late/missing/contradictory event, restart/recovery, unexpected stock draw and planned arrival. Maintain a seed+fixture+schedule for exact reruns; the judge may alter fault time/amount or manual measurement spontaneously.

**Emulator test:** Same seed/config/schedule yields identical byte-level event payloads (ignore nondeterministic delivery metadata); different hidden-parameter settings can cause Projection Check disagreement; wrong timestamps/units are caught. Ensure emulator cannot mutate the forecast model's calibration parameters behind the scenes.

### 6.2 Two profiles, not one invented station
**Bharati:** dated NCPOR tender identifies three 100-kVA CHP units, Jet-A1 supply and seawater intake [S03]. Use these only as `DOCUMENTED_HISTORICAL_INFRASTRUCTURE`. Generator dispatchable kW, fuel/heat curves, actual operating rotation, current fuel and thresholds are `UNKNOWN` or explicit `DEMO_ASSUMPTION_RANGE`. **Maitri:** public NCPOR station page documents a lake-water pump house and different site topology [S02]; current generator topology/coefficients remain unknown. Both use the same calculation *library*, separate manifest/dependency graphs and separate parameter ledgers. A scenario needing unknown parameters must ask user to instantiate a demo fixture and label outputs **illustrative**.

### 6.3 Minimum causal solver
Hourly state: available dispatchable kW; nondeferrable/deferrable electric demand; fuel L; indoor/outside °C; CHP recoverable heat and auxiliary heat; water buffer L, water-plant/intake status; stock and future delivery interval. Numerical timestep configurable for convergence tests.

- `gap_kW=max(0,critical_kW+deferrable_kW+electric_aux_heat_kW−available_kW)`; dispatch never exceeds explicitly assumed/observed kW. Installed 100 kVA is **not** 100 kW without a declared factor.
- `fuel_next_L=max(0,fuel_L−Δt_h·Σburn_i(dispatch_kW)−Δt_h·other_burn_Lph)` and separately record demand not served. Never infer current burn from an old annual total.
- `C_kWh_per_C·(Tin_next−Tin)/Δt_h=Q_CHP(dispatch)+Q_aux−UA_kW_per_C·(Tin−Tout)`. Electric aux heat is paid for in electric load; other fuel paid for in fuel balance. No fabricated pipe-freeze time.
- `water_next_L=max(0,water_L+Δt_h·(production_Lph−demand_Lph))`; production depends on source/intake/plant/power; shortages recorded.
- `stock_next=stock+entered_receipts−entered_use`; the next replenishment window is user-entered, uncertain and route-specific, not automatically inferred from continent-wide sea ice.

State snapshot (`revision_hash`, event high-water marks, manifest/model versions and evidence IDs) is immutable. Scenario forks never create observations. Time-to-threshold means crossing a **demo-configured modeled constraint**; not station human survival. Parameter sweeps provide ranges, not calibrated failure probabilities. Limit P0 to fuel/water and one CHP→heating cascade; generic manually entered food/spare threshold optional P1.

### 6.4 The three differentiators as algorithms

| Feature | Input → algorithm → output | Demonstration, limitation and validation |
|---|---|---|
| **Measure Next** | Assumption ranges, measurable unknowns, candidate actions → vary parameters (including interactions where feasible) and detect action flips/constraint changes → ranked **measurement request with why** | Widen backup capacity range, choose unit-kW check; after station reports value, plan changes. Only ranks declared unknowns; no universal optimum. Unit/property tests and seeded scenario cases. |
| **Robust Action Check** | Snapshot, finite candidate plans, constraints and parameter/weather ensemble → run each case, reject any violating configured hard constraint; retain counterexample → `ROBUST_IN_TESTED_RANGE`, `CONDITIONAL`, `INFEASIBLE_IN_MODEL`, `INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE` | A fuel-saving load/heat strategy fails room-temperature threshold. Sampled scenarios not all possible physics; don't call it guaranteed safe. Analytical/conservation and counterexample tests. |
| **Projection Check** | HQ saved conditional envelope+revision+target time and later station event → reconcile, time-align, unit/quality-check, compare → `WITHIN`, `OUTSIDE`, `NOT_COMPARABLE`, invalidate forecast and trigger measurement/inspection request if justified | Emulator hidden perturbation yields unexplained deviation; time-mismatched event is NOT_COMPARABLE. Neither result validates real station or diagnoses leak. Record interval width and comparison count, not just in-range rate. |

**Recommended order:** robust candidate validation and task requests P0; full sensitivity-ranked Measure Next and Projection Check P1 if time tight, but minimal projection comparison should be shown in final end-to-end demo. Unknown parameter without defensible range → abstain, not precision theater.

## 7. Data taxonomy and lineage

| Class | Example | Display label / prohibited inference |
|---|---|---|
| Official dated fact | 2013 Bharati tender CHP nameplates | `DOCUMENTED (2013)` / not current kW or stock |
| Historical observation | NPDC Maitri/Bharati weather timestamp | `HISTORICAL WEATHER` / not today's environment |
| External science data | AntAWS; ERA5 reanalysis | `HISTORICAL EXTERNAL`; `REANALYSIS` / not same as in-situ reading |
| Demo replay fixture | Bundled historical slice or test tape | `REPLAY` with original clock, license |
| Synthetic | Emulated fuel or unit availability | `DEMO SYNTHETIC` / not Antarctic instrument data |
| Manual | Station-role entered fuel level | `MANUAL DEMO ENTRY` until source verified |
| Model-derived | thermal trajectory or forecast | `PROJECTED (CONDITIONAL)` / not observation |
| Unavailable | actual present fuel reserve | `UNKNOWN` / suppress dependent precise output |

**Lineage:** `source URL/licence or emulator fixture → timestamp/unit/quality validator → append-only event ID/source seq → observed/reported reducer revision → model manifest + assumption ledger → scenario ID/seed → action feasibility → plan/task ID → station response ID → HQ review/projection comparison`. Store this chain in a provenance drawer, CSV/JSON export and a human-readable incident brief.

**Important nuance:** The emulator may use a real *historical outdoor weather input* while its generator, fuel and indoor temperature remain synthetic. A composite value must display both contributors; calling the full stream “real station data” is prohibited. Public NPDC weather file access, redistribution rights and exact coverage need individual verification [S06]; fallback to a small explicitly synthetic offline weather fixture, not unlicensed data.

### 8.1 Final system architecture diagram

                                └─> local Operations Manager + station UI
                                                │       ▲
                          station reports/tasks │       │ ground proposals
                                                v       │
                                  sync gateway / actual blocked HTTP transport
                                                │
                                                v

                                │                  │

### 8.2 Data flow diagram

```text
input [origin + date + unit + quality] → validated station event → journal
→ snapshot revision → immutable scenario fork + weather/parameter ranges
→ candidate constraint result → human-created proposal/task with expected conditional outcome
→ queued transport → station receipt → local user disposition and manual/synthetic report
→ persisted edge event → reconnect and dedupe → HQ reviewed outcome
→ compare saved projection with time-matched returning event → invalidate/recompute → audit
```

### 8.3 Deployment and cost
Use **one laptop** with three localhost services (emulator + station edge(s) + HQ) and one UI application with explicit station/HQ roles; use separate SQLite files so blocking *only the sync gateway* actually prevents delivery without stopping edge HTTP/UI. Avoid a UI toggle that merely changes label. Python FastAPI/Pydantic and SQLite WAL; React/TypeScript+SVG or simpler server-rendered UI; pytest/property tests. One-command offline start after dependencies installed; bundle local assets. A simulated network link may be a gateway process that refuses batch requests, queues and later delivers them; no physical cable or Internet needed. Persist state before ACK. Optional Docker Compose, but normal Python/Node scripts must work if Docker isn't installed. No paid cloud required.

### 8.4 Event contract and bidirectional streams
`event_id=origin_node/source_id/monotonic_seq`, `kind`, `schema_version`, `created_at_utc`, `observed_at_utc` if a measurement, `received_at_utc`, `station`, `payload`, `source_kind`, `unit`, `quality`, `causation_id`, `correlation_id`, `actor`, `base_revision`, `payload_hash`. Do not claim hashes authenticate origin; prototype auth can use a demo shared secret, with explicit production caveats. HQ-owned plan events and edge-owned observations/replies live in **separate streams**. Sync ACKs highest persisted contiguous sequence per source; gap buffers and duplicate IDs; invalid payload quarantined. Ensure plan delivery receipt is *distinct* from human `SEEN` and `ACCEPTED`. Retry with backoff; bounded queue and retention warnings. Results from different model versions cannot be silently compared. Timestamps in UTC and their origin/clock-quality explicit.

### 8.5 Mode-by-mode behavior

| Mode | Edge | HQ | Invariant |
|---|---|---|---|
| NORMAL | stores local observations, receives queued proposals; local user responds | sends proposals, receives ACK/outcomes | all writes journaled; HQ never overwrites edge facts |
| OFFLINE | continues emulator ingest, local scenarios/tasks, records human response | observed view freezes with age/gap label; proposal remains queued | no phantom delivery, no HQ knowledge of new events |
| RECONNECT | replays valid events, quarantines bad ones; receives queued proposals | dedupes, merges owned streams, invalidates stale predictions | old plan base revision checked locally before display |
| CONFLICT | retains local task disposition and both plan revisions | shows both concurrent planning edits | no last-write-wins on operational plan |
| FAILURE | alerts for journal/disk/service/clock problems; no silent success | marks edge unknown/stale | no outcome/ACK without durable commit |
| RECOVERY | restarts from WAL, rebuilds reducer, resends unacknowledged batch | recomputes reports/projections | convergence verified after all valid events delivered |

**Conflicts:** HQ cannot edit edge measurements. Same-plan concurrent edits at HQ and edge become sibling revisions requiring manual merge. Separate independently named plan notes can merge; accepted proposal amendments require a new version and local acknowledgement. Expired proposals are marked, not automatically executed. Local station event causality uses per-source sequence; cross-source clock order is not authoritative.

**Screens:**
1. **Fleet:** HQ receives station status + freshness, plan backlog, first modeled constraint only if calculable; state source badge.
2. **Station Twin:** 2D causal schematic and asset provenance; current demo-reported vs projected separated.

## 10. AI strategy and security boundary

**AI is not required for the core.** Deterministic conservation equations + parameter sweeps + a finite action set give testable results. A rolling median/MAD can flag unusual *demo* trends but cannot diagnose a real generator. An optional local language model may summarize structured, verified output in plain language; it must cite IDs, refuse unknowns, and be fully replaceable by deterministic templates. It cannot create task instructions, numerical facts, thresholds or medical/safety advice. Cloud inference is excluded from offline operations. Predictive-maintenance RUL is excluded until approved labeled station failure histories exist; NASA aircraft turbofan data are not proof of transfer [S20].

**Safety boundary:** `external approved source (read-only) → digital state + modeled possibilities → human-to-human proposal/task → local human checks applicable authorized SOP → separately recorded physical action, if any`. There is **no** `POST /generator`, `/breaker`, `/pump`, `/actuate`, shadow `desired` control field or machine-readable setpoint. A station user clicking accepted signifies receipt/review of a proposal, **not** industrial safety approval. If human executes something wrong, software cannot guarantee protection; its only defenses are transparency, stale/unknown warnings, no direct actuation and a requirement for independent verification. Future industrial control requires a separate NCPOR-authorized safety/security project, hazard assessment, field interlocks and trials; no promise that this MVP simply toggles that capability on.

**Threat model P0:** malicious plan spoofing, replay/duplicate events, privilege confusion, stale task accepted after disconnection, altered unit/time, payload injection, dataset tampering, disk saturation, sensitive data leakage. Validate schema/units; demo auth per role, HTTPS when exposed, separate database/permissions, signed or MAC'd batch integrity **only when implemented**, nonce/sequence replay protection, explicit expiries, no secrets in repo. Prototype is not certified and should not process restricted station/ISRO data.

## 11. Validation, tests and demo realism

### 11.1 Three different validation claims
1. **Software verification:** run analytic constant fuel-burn and one-zone exponential thermal special cases; conservation and invariants; correct plan state transitions and event replay. This is achievable now.
2. **Emulator holdout:** tune no decision-model parameter on hidden emulator test seed/fault segment, compare projection envelopes vs later emulated readings; report discrepancy detection and width. This validates a *software-in-the-loop exercise*, **not** a real station.
3. **Operational validation:** only after approved site logs, parameter estimation on train periods, holdout seasons/assets, operator review and safety/security assessment. **Not achieved.** NIST's VVUQ emphasis supports this separation [S10].

### 11.2 Automated acceptance tests
- Same emulator fixture/seed yields stable synthetic source sequences; model fork doesn't mutate journal.
- Constant fuel burn → analytical time threshold; thermal model → closed-form constant-forcing solution, timestep convergence; no dispatch beyond available **kW**, no CHP heat when unavailable, no uncharged auxiliary heat; water cannot be produced without configured source/plant/power.
- Missing fuel/current load produces `INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE`, not precise endurance. Conditional action with a counterexample is not labelled robust. Measure Next ranks a parameter that actually changes a decision in seeded test.
- A plan in HQ `QUEUED` is absent from edge during real transport block; reconnect persists/ACKs/dedupes and edge shows `DELIVERED`; manual station response returns later; duplicate ACK cannot create double stock adjustment.
- Source out-of-order/gaps, restart, clock skew, invalid signature/MAC/hash, schema mismatch, plan expiry and concurrent edits are handled visibly. A stale proposed plan is not auto-accepted. Eventual event-set equality is asserted **only after all valid events arrive and conflicts resolved**.
- Projection Check returns `NOT_COMPARABLE` for mismatched timestamp, unit, quality or model version; hidden emulator perturbation may produce `OUTSIDE`; unknown cause remains unknown.
- HQ projection never presented as observation, demo never labelled live, no actuator API endpoints in route inventory.

### 11.3 Actual measurements to gather
Local scenario runtime, event queue size at given rate/outage, bytes sent including protocol overhead, reconnect time, CPU/RAM, optional edge device watts, task completion/acceptance workflow timing in a small usability rehearsal. Fix test machine and conditions. Targets (e.g. sub-2-second 48-hour run) are aspirations until measured. No statistical impact or safety outcome claims based on synthetic data.

## 12. Five-minute judge script (entirely offline laptop)

| Time | Action | Observable proof |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:30 | Show two profiles and evidence badges; disclose emulator and historic weather | no live-station pretense |
| 0:30–1:10 | Judge edits load or ambient temperature, then injects CHP unavailable | real edge event, revised snapshot and causal power/heat/water result |
| 1:10–1:55 | HQ runs scenario, compares load adjustment/backup hypothesis; show first constraint and counterexample | twin behavior + Robust Action Check |
| 1:55–2:35 | Measure Next asks for available backup kW or fuel verification; HQ creates request and action-plan proposal linked to scenario revision | **remote management object**, not prose recommendation |
| 2:35–3:05 | Open station inbox on second UI; show delivered, manually accept/decline and enter a checked reading. Block gateway before a second message | clear proposal versus station decision and actual link failure |
| 3:05–3:45 | While offline edge keeps journaling; station user reports a new observation and task outcome; HQ view stays timestamped/stale and its new request queued | local continuity without fake delivery |
| 3:45–4:30 | Reconnect; watch ACKs/dedupe, stale-plan check, receipt, reported outcome and projection comparison. Hidden emulator perturbation can produce disagreement | reconciliation, outcome learning, no unsupported cause diagnosis |
| 4:30–5:00 | Open event trace and tests; state what is synthetic/dated and what was verified | credible limitation and reproducible evidence |

**Control rules:** No prerecording presented as live; all model inputs editable; single laptop services use separate storage; network blocker stops only the sync gateway, not local UI. If historic weather licensing/access fails, announce and use a synthetic weather fixture; label all synthetic station readings explicitly. The station-side role may be a teammate, not an auto-accept bot. A separate optional ESP32 bench temperature sensor only demonstrates the ingestion adapter and is omitted if unstable.

**Failure cases to rehearse:** edge restart while offline; HQ plan expires before delivery; station rejects proposal; late contradictory reading has BAD quality; HQ and station change same plan; supply arrival assumption moves; no action meets critical power; missing fuel; emulator hidden parameter drift; sync disk near cap. Use at least one unfamiliar judge-selected input.

## 13. Judge's critical scorecard (not official numeric weights)

| Criterion | What the working build proves | What it cannot prove / judge question |
|---|---|---|
| Novelty | linked evidence-sensitive scenario → managed task → reconciled outcome | others may have workflows; do not say first [S21–S23] |
| Complexity | station-owned events, plan lifecycle, constraint solver, provenance | not industrial controller; show real tests not stack diagram |
| Clarity | one coherent incident and obvious observed/synthetic/projected badges | avoid overloading UI with all optional modules |
| Feasibility | three local processes, SQLite, finite model, no hardware/cloud dependency | current station data not available; keep demo fixture honest |
| Practicability | station can reject stale plan; HQ obtains actual response | NCPOR workflows/authority need discovery; may need integration with existing apps [S04][S05] |
| Sustainability | bounded queues, replay, separate manifests, data/parameter versions | real bandwidth, disk, watts and production security unmeasured |
| Impact | prospective improved coordination/avoided unjustified proposals | no proven fuel savings/incident prevention |
| UX | clear next owner, task status, uncertainty and local station inbox | test with unfamiliar participants and accessible controls |
| Progression | read-only pilot then approved data calibration | real deployment requires NCPOR review, SOP alignment and validation |

**Self-attack answers:**
- “Just a dashboard?” No: create a versioned proposal, deliver it through a genuinely blockable link, receive a human disposition and report, and close/reopen based on an outcome.
- “Fake sensors?” Yes, **explicitly** emulated; only public weather is historical. Software behavior is demonstrable, station operational accuracy is not.
- “Not a digital twin?” Revisioned state + behavior + forked scenario + later reconciliation exist; **twin of emulated station** is tested, real stations require calibration.
- “Model fabricated?” Parameters are sourced/unknown/demo-assumed; bounds and abstentions are visible; independent analytic tests prove implementation only.
- “Wrong model?” Show a projection discrepancy, retract stale plan and ask for a new measurement; no actuator path.
- “Internet fails?” edge and station tasks continue locally, HQ remains stale, durable streams reconcile when valid delivery resumes, bounded by disk/power.
- “Why no direct control?” Physical plant safety, authorization and cybersecurity would require a separate approved system; remote human workflow **is** legitimate management.
- “How is this different from existing NCPOR tools?” Their public descriptions indicate issue and logistics management [S04][S05]; PRATIBIMB's proposed complement is a **traceable model-supported plan and offline outcome reconciliation**. Actual product overlap/APIs are not established.

## 14. Competitors and differentiator

Public SIH repositories already claim 3D station twins, causal simulations, edge/offline queues, logistics and AI: PolarCore [S21], Himantar [S22], POLAR-OPS [S23]. This does not claim unique invention or real-station accuracy.” To claim another team's deficiency, freeze a commit hash, run their comparable scenario, and document reproducible observations; otherwise say only what its public README **claims**.

## 17. Why V2 solves PS 26060 better

PRATIBIMB covers the PS's four named domains through **one inspectable causal state**, serves **both** stations through distinct manifests, and makes **remote management tangible** by moving a proposed plan from HQ to a human at the station and receiving a traceable response back. It has a **real emulator/replay boundary** so a laptop can demonstrate all transitions without borrowing the prestige of imaginary Antarctic sensors. Local computation persists through a genuinely blocked network; reconnection delivers and reconciles events, with a separate prediction-versus-observation check. The system never mistakes scenario feasibility for operational authority, and explicitly tells users when evidence is insufficient. That is the best defensible MVP: **not real station automation, but a working, auditable framework for remote operational coordination and decision rehearsal that could be calibrated and integrated only after NCPOR authorization.**
